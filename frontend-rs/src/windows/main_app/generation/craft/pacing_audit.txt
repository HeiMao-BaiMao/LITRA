Audit the pacing and information disclosure of the supplied fiction draft, in reading order.

WHAT TO MEASURE:
1. Velocity map: divide the draft into consecutive segments. For each segment, judge the narrative speed (fast: action or rapid dialogue; medium; slow: description or reflection) and whether that speed fits what the segment is trying to do.
2. Disclosure order: list the key pieces of information the reader needs, and where each arrives. Flag each as: too-early (kills tension), too-late (causes confusion), never (dropped), or well-placed.
3. Tension curve: identify where tension rises, holds, and releases. Flag long holds with no change, and releases that arrive before the reader has invested anything.

NOT ISSUES:
- Deliberate slow passages that pay off within the draft.
- Withheld information that is clearly a planted question (a promise), unless it causes confusion at this point in the reading.
- Stylistic density that matches the surrounding manuscript's established mode.
